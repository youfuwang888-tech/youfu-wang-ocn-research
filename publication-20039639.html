Platform Integral as a Cross-System Mechanism for Window Formation in Hybrid Mode Systems

Wang, Youfu

Publication date: 2026-05-05 | DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.20039639

Photonics, slow light, and spectral windows

Abstract

Stable transmission or propagation windows are widely observed in hybrid mode systems, including polaritonic and photonic crystal structures. Conventional interpretations attribute these windows to local extrema, such as minimum residue or maximum group index. However, such pointwise descriptions do not fully explain variations in window width or consistency across systems. &nbsp; In this work, we propose a platform integral framework for window formation. Instead of relying on local extrema, we introduce a global measure based on the structure of system-dependent landscapes: &nbsp; P^* = \int \exp(-X/\langle X\rangle)\cdot \exp(-|\nabla X|/\langle|\nabla X|\rangle)\, d\Omega &nbsp; where X represents a system-specific variable, such as residue Z_R in polariton systems or group velocity v_g in photonic crystal waveguides. &nbsp; Across systems, a consistent principle emerges: local extrema determine the central position of the window, while the integrated weight of low-value plateaus governs the usable window width. &nbsp; This framework is supported by data-driven results in polariton systems and is consistent with observations of flatband slow-light modes in photonic crystal waveguides. &nbsp; These results suggest a cross-system structural mechanism for window formation based on platform integrals, providing a unified perspective beyond extremum-based interpretations.
