From Structure to Behavior: Role-Semantic Invariants as the Basis of Behavioral Mode Generation

Wang, Youfu

Publication date: 2026-03-26 | DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.19227888

Runtime governance and structure-constrained behavior

Abstract

We propose a theoretical framework for behavior generation in dynamical systems, termed Structure-Constrained Behavior Generation (SCBG). &nbsp; Unlike conventional approaches in control and learning, where behavior is derived from optimization or learned policies, SCBG suggests that behavior emerges as a constrained selection process defined by structural invariants. &nbsp; A key contribution of this work is the introduction of role-semantic structural invariants, which extend traditional graph-based representations by incorporating functional roles associated with nodes and pathways. Within this framework, behavior is determined not by raw connectivity or parameter values, but by invariant structural relations including opposition, closure, hierarchical pathways, and role semantics. &nbsp; Through a minimal experimental evidence chain, we show that: &nbsp; structural differences induce distinct behavioral modes, perturbations of structural invariants lead to behavioral degradation, structural influence dominates input mapping, and behavior equivalence is preserved only under role-consistent structural transformations within a bounded scaling regime. &nbsp; &nbsp; These results support a shift from policy-driven behavior generation toward structure-driven mode selection, suggesting a new perspective for understanding behavior in dynamical systems, robotics, and control.
