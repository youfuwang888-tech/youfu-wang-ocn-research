A Qubit No-Go Bound for Independent Commuting Finite-Order Controls

Wang, Youfu

2026-09-12 | Preprint / author manuscript; not a journal publication | DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.22718575

Abstract / source description

Two finite-order quantum controls may each be compatible with a qubit while their joint algebra is not. We study a cyclic control Q and an independent involution R with target algebra C_n x C_2. For even n >= 4, no faithful projective qubit realization exists: in PU(2) ≃ SO(3), a nontrivial involution commuting with an order-n rotation collapses to Q^(n/2). An explicit qutrit construction realizes the full algebra, so the minimum projective dimension is three; n = 4 is the first obstructed even member and has an eight-ray regular orbit. The representation-theory obstruction itself lies in a mature mathematical area. The operational contribution developed here is a calibrated qubit-consistency test: for exact quarter-turn and half-turn targets, the commutator angle gives a tight state-independent fidelity bound and a tight optimized binary-contrast cap. We derive a sufficient angular-error version and extend the test to general CPTP gate deviations through diamond distance. The explicit qutrit construction reaches the algebraic maximum binary contrast of one using two control sequences and a common binary measurement. The result is a calibrated falsification test for a qubit control model, not a device-independent dimension witness, and it remains entirely within standard finite-dimensional quantum mechanics.
