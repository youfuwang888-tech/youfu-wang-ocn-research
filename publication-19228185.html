SCBG Guardian: A Structure-Constrained Runtime Safety Layer for Behavior Stabilization

王有福

Publication date: 2026-03-26 | DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.19228185

Abstract

We introduce SCBG Guardian, a runtime safety layer for dynamical systems that stabilizes behavior through structure-constrained mode selection. &nbsp; Unlike conventional safety mechanisms based on threshold-triggered rules or constraint enforcement, SCBG Guardian operates by monitoring system states and dynamically switching between behavioral modes such as approach, avoidance, and stabilization. &nbsp; The key idea is that safety is not enforced through fixed constraints, but emerges from transitions between structurally valid behavior modes defined by role-semantic invariants. &nbsp; We evaluate SCBG Guardian in a minimal simulation environment and compare it against: (1) a nominal controller and (2) a threshold-based safety mechanism. &nbsp; The results show that: &nbsp; the nominal controller may achieve task objectives but fails due to collisions or instability, threshold-based methods avoid collisions but often lead to stalled or overly conservative behavior, SCBG Guardian avoids collisions while maintaining task progression through dynamic mode transitions. &nbsp; &nbsp; These findings suggest that behavioral safety can be reframed as a structure-driven mode selection problem, rather than a constraint enforcement problem.
