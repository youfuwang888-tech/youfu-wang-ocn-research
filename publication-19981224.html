Protocol-Dependent Optimality in Near-Edge Systems: A Unified Explanation for Experimental Discrepancies in Slow-Light Regimes

王有福

Publication date: 2026-05-02 | DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.19981224

Abstract

This work presents a unified framework for understanding optimal operating points in near-edge physical systems, with a focus on slow-light photonic crystal waveguides. &nbsp; By introducing a Difference Explanation Model (DEM), we show that system performance can be described as a balance between enhancement (gain) and degradation (loss), where the latter is strongly influenced by experimental protocols such as coupling configuration, polarization, and mode selection. &nbsp; The framework explains why similar structures can exhibit significantly different optimal operating points, and provides a predictive expression for the shift of the optimal point under different protocols. The model is supported by existing experimental observations in slow-light coupling and nonlinear enhancement systems. &nbsp; This work aims to provide a minimal, testable criterion for analyzing near-edge systems, without replacing existing theories, but offering a higher-level explanation for experimental variability.
