From Necessary Conditions to Binary Observation: A Working Hypothesis for a 3–8–2 Hierarchical Generative Framework

Wang, Youfu

Publication date: 2026-09-02 | DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.22239705

Quantum foundations and binary observation

Abstract

This paper proposes an exploratory &ldquo;3&ndash;8&ndash;2&rdquo; hierarchical generative framework designed to distinguish three different logical layers: a necessary-condition layer, a generative-process layer, and an observational-output layer. The current working hypothesis is: 3\ \text{necessary conditions} \rightarrow 8\ \text{candidate generative processes} \rightarrow 2\ \text{binary observational outcomes}. At the first layer, &ldquo;Yuan&rdquo; is provisionally defined as the minimal closed condition structure formed by three mutually irreducible and jointly necessary conditions. The number 3 therefore refers to the number of necessary conditions rather than to a number of physical states. At the second layer, the number 8 refers to eight candidate generative processes, process configurations, or generative pathways that may emerge once the necessary conditions are satisfied. Their precise mathematical origin and physical interpretation remain open. In particular, this framework does not assume that these eight processes correspond to an established eight-state quantum ontology. At the third layer, the number 2 refers to binary observational outcomes, represented as 0/1, obtained through coarse-graining, projection, or limited observational resolution. These binary outputs are therefore treated as observational results rather than as evidence that the underlying ontology is fundamentally binary. The aim of this work is not to claim a new physical ontology, but to formulate a testable hierarchical research framework that clearly separates the conditions required for a process to exist, the mechanisms through which structures or processes are generated, and the outcomes accessible to observation. The central open problem is to identify the three irreducible necessary conditions that constitute &ldquo;Yuan&rdquo; and to determine whether the minimum number of such conditions is genuinely three rather than two, four, or more. At its present stage, the proposed framework should be regarded as a working hypothesis and structural research program. Its possible connections to quantum processes, hidden structures, and physical realizations remain open and unproven.
