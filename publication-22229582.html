Observer-Relative Undifferentiated Probability Envelopes: Operational Equivalence, Ontological Underdetermination, and the First-Distinction Boundary

Wang, Youfu

Publication date: 2026-09-01 | DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.22229582

Quantum foundations and binary observation

Abstract

This preprint introduces the concept of an observer-relative undifferentiated probability envelope(观察者相对的未分化概率包络) as a minimal operational description for situations in which a family of candidate underlying states cannot yet be distinguished by a specified observer, interface, or intervention set. Rather than treating &ldquo;undifferentiated&rdquo; as an absolute absence of structure, the framework defines it through an observer-induced equivalence relation on a candidate state space. When all candidate states belong to a single operational equivalence class, the observer has access only to a coarse probability-level description. The paper distinguishes two interpretations that must remain separate. Model A(模型A), adopted as the current operational description, treats the probability distribution itself as the minimal empirically justified state description. Model B(模型B) is retained as an open ontological hypothesis in which each individual realization may already possess a unique underlying actual state, while the observer has only incomplete knowledge of that state. Model B is neither accepted nor rejected; it is reserved for future tests that could produce predictions distinguishable from Model A. The analysis formalizes the first-distinction boundary using equivalence classes and partition refinement, shows that a minimal nontrivial refinement naturally produces two distinguishable classes without implying physical binary ontology, and emphasizes the distinction between operational indistinguishability and ontological identity. Quantum coherent superposition is deliberately not assumed as the deepest interpretation of the undifferentiated layer. Instead, quantum-state descriptions are treated provisionally as higher-level effective operational representations whose deeper interpretation remains open. This work freezes a temporary lower boundary in a broader research chain connecting observer-relative distinguishability, admissible state spaces, relational structure, dynamics, projection, coarse-graining, and finite observational readout. The next stage is to determine whether a deeper ontological completion can generate experimentally distinguishable consequences, or whether the probability-envelope description is operationally complete.
