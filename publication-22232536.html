Yuan (元), Primitive Distinction, and the Operational Descriptive Horizon: A Frozen Coarse-to-Fine Research Chain

Wang, Youfu

Publication date: 2026-09-01 | DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.22232536

Other public research records

Abstract

This structural preprint freezes the current lower boundary reached in a continuing coarse-to-fine research program on observation, distinguishability, probability, admissible state spaces, dynamics, and finite readout. The paper introduces the provisional Chinese term Yuan (元) solely as a low-commitment placeholder for the presently unresolved side of the first expressible distinction. Yuan is not proposed as a state, object, field, observer, intrinsic capacity, or physical entity, and its existence is not asserted. The first layer that can currently be formalized is a relational distinction: a test, comparison, interaction, or reference yields at least two operationally different responses. From this layer the paper freezes a chain through primitive cut, event algebra, observer/test family, probability envelope, observer-resolution refinement, latent-state disclosure, relational/admissible state structure, dynamics/flow, projection/coarse-graining, and finite readout. The operational descriptive horizon is placed between Yuan and the first mathematically expressible relational distinction. Below that horizon, assigning a property to Yuan already reintroduces a distinction; above it, distinctions can be represented and tested. The horizon is therefore a limit of present descriptive resolution, not a claim that reality ends there. Quantum coherent superposition remains PARKED / UNMAPPED, and an observer-independent unique-actual-state hypothesis remains OPEN / UNPROVEN.
